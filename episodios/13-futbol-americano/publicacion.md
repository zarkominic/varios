## Descripción (EN)

On November 1, 1913, at West Point, a little-known school called Notre Dame faced mighty Army, and its quarterback, Gus Dorais, kept throwing long spiral passes to Knute Rockne. Notre Dame won 35–13, and the forward pass became famous overnight. Today every quarterback throws that same pass: a ball spinning about 600 times a minute that tips its nose down halfway through its flight. How does it do that, and why is the ball shaped so strangely?

What we cover:

- Ada, Ohio: the small town where every NFL ball is made by hand;
- the kick: a hundredth of a second, thin air in Denver, and Cam Little's 68-yard record;
