## Descripción (EN)

In 1922, Howard Carter looked through a small hole by candlelight and saw "wonderful things." More than a hundred years
later, King Tut's tomb may still be hiding rooms nobody has opened. This is the story of a tomb too small for a king,
straight lines under the paint, three radar scans that couldn't agree, and a team that wants to drill a hole three
inches wide to find out.

What we cover:
- 1922: the last season, the first step, and a candle at the second door;
- why the tomb is so small, and why things in it carry another name;
- the newspapers that invented a curse;
- 2015: an exact copy of the burial chamber, and the lines under the paint;
- Nefertiti, the queen whose tomb has never been found;
- three radars, three answers, and a case declared closed;
- 2020: a scan from the hillside, and a corridor to the east;
- 2026: weighing the mountain, and patches with right-angled corners;
- why a small drill hole, and not knocking down the wall;
- what measuring can say, and what only looking can.
