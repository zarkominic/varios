In 1958, the US Air Force studied setting off an atomic bomb on the Moon, just so the whole world could see the flash.
It sounds like a conspiracy theory, but the physicist who led the study told the story himself in 2000, and one of his
young number crunchers was Carl Sagan. So we opened six secret files that sound like a joke and are completely real.

What we cover:
- Project A119: Sputnik, the Moon plan in Chicago, a young Carl Sagan, and how it came out forty years later;
- the animal spies: the CIA's Acoustic Kitty, B. F. Skinner's pigeon-guided bomb, and Blue Peacock, the nuclear mine
  kept warm by chickens (released on April Fools' Day);
- the psychic spies: remote viewing at Stanford and the Stargate program;
- the Ghost Army: inflatable tanks, fake sounds, the Rhine deception and the 2024 Gold Medal;
- Project Habakkuk: the aircraft carrier made of ice, pykrete, and the prototype at the bottom of Patricia Lake.
