## Descripción (EN)

In 1985, Coca-Cola changed the recipe of the most famous soft drink in the world. It had the science on its side: about
two hundred thousand blind taste tests said the new one was better. Seventy-nine days later, after a storm of phone
calls, letters and protests, the company had to bring the old one back.

What we cover:
- 1886: John Pemberton, a pharmacy soda fountain in Atlanta, and nine glasses a day;
- Asa Candler, the secret formula in a bank vault, and the bottling rights sold for one dollar;
- the Pepsi Challenge, unmarked cups and a market share that kept slipping;
- Roberto Goizueta and Project Kansas;
- April 23, 1985: the press conference at Lincoln Center, and Pepsi's day off;
- more than forty thousand calls and letters, a psychiatrist, and Gay Mullins in Seattle;
- July 11, 1985: Coca-Cola Classic;
- why the taste tests got it wrong: sips, blind cups, and the 2004 brain scanner study.
